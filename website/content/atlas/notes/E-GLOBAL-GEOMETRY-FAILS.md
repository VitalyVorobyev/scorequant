The smallest-eigenvalue criterion is the natural choice when a guarantee is needed for every parameter direction rather than for the volume, but it has no exchange-implies-geometry theorem: an exhaustively searched finite example with a simple minimum eigenvalue has a global optimum whose own rank-one nearest-cell rule disagrees with a training label. This is why there is no E-optimality criterion implemented; the omission traces to this counterexample rather than to a gap in coverage.

The witness is floating-point rather than exact rational, unlike most of the other counterexamples discussed here, and no prior-art search is recorded for it, so it stands as a witness rather than an attribution claim.
